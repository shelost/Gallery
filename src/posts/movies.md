These are the movies I keep coming back to. There's no real theme here — just the ones I'll happily rewatch and end up recommending to anyone who'll listen. I'll keep adding to the list as I find more.


## Vice (2018)

<iframe width="420" height="360"
src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/g09a9laLh0k">
</iframe>

This is a pretty uncommon pick, but I genuinely think *Vice* is one of the best-directed and most tightly written movies of the 21st century. The way it cuts between dry corporate boredom and the enormous consequences of those quiet decisions still gets me every time.


## Le Comte de Monte Cristo (2024)

<iframe width="420" height="360"
src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/8Q6y1waxlTY">
</iframe>

Listen, I've watched every "Count of Monte Cristo" adaptation imaginable. I've even sat through the [1975 English TV film](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mRiqixYweNQ) starring Richard Chamberlain, which still somehow beats the [2002 American adaptation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qesn8pV9yu8) starring Jim Caviezel (the worst version, and I'm not arguing with anyone about that). This French one finally does the book justice.


## Uprising (2024)

<iframe width="420" height="360"
src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/1aEjAxJtw2U">
</iframe>

This is a Korean film on Netflix, set in the wreckage left behind by the Imjin War. Two boys grow up together — one the young master, one his slave — and the movie picks them back up years later, on opposite sides of a war, in a country that decided who they were the moment they were born.

What I love about it is that it never lets you forget that last part. The injustice isn't a backdrop; it's the whole engine of the story. A person's worth was settled at birth, and the film is furious about it in a way that feels earned rather than preachy. It gives you the catharsis you want from a revenge story without ever pretending the cruelty was an accident — it was the system working exactly as intended. For all the spectacle, it's really asking a simple question: who gets to be free, and who pays for it?
